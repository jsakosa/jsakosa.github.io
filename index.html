About me

I build the method, then explain it plainly.

My work sits where rigorous inference meets a real decision: who to treat, who to contact, which signal is worth chasing.

I'm a Ph.D.-trained biostatistician and statistical scientist. My specialties are high-dimensional inference, false discovery rate control, predictive modeling, and classification when the outcome you care about is rare.

An internship at Nationwide Insurance showed me how badly accuracy can mislead on imbalanced data. That question became a paper that has been cited more than 400 times. Since 2019 I've been at the University of Notre Dame, partnering with researchers on study design and analysis, building healthcare analytics projects on real clinical data, and teaching statistics to students who don't think of themselves as math people.

I'm now bringing that mix to an industry team: sound methods, reproducible code, and results a stakeholder can act on.

Featured · 400+ citations

Predictive accuracy is a misleading measure for highly imbalanced data

When only a sliver of cases are positive, a model can look excellent by never predicting a positive at all. This paper showed why and compared measures that hold up better: sensitivity, specificity, F-measure, G-mean, and AUC. It grew out of a real modeling problem at Nationwide.
